Ankura Homes · Iqon West

Villa UX for living experience — enhancing how residents live, move, and feel within their spaces. Because a home should feel designed for you, not just built around you.

  • Iqon West project
  • Ankura Homes

Problems identified

Everyday villa life was fighting friction in the details — infrastructure, materials, washrooms, landscape intent, and how families first experienced the site.

  • Visible water and electricity pipelines broke visual harmony on villa façades while still needing easy maintenance access.

  • Door choices in wet and utility areas traded off durability, termite risk, lifecycle cost, and maintenance against look and feel.

  • Washrooms felt closed-in: dark or mismatched tones, and short faucets that splash and frustrate daily use.

  • Landscaping was treated as decoration rather than part of everyday experience — missing sensory, medicinal, aromatic, and productive value.

  • Sales visits lacked a strong first impression (incomplete model villa display) and did not consistently prioritize belonging for elderly visitors and women of the family.

Research

User experience in architecture goes beyond aesthetics — it is how people experience a space every day. The goal is to blend functionality, aesthetics, and comfort into a seamless living environment. Every detail matters: from how morning light filters through windows to how landscaping frames the view.

  • Invisible utilities

    Reduce visual noise without blocking maintenance access.

  • Doors

    Compared wood vs PVC across durability, maintenance, cost, sustainability, and look — PVC stronger for wet zones.

  • Washrooms

    Light tones, matte anti-skid tiles, and matched wall/floor tones expand perceived space.

  • Fixtures

    Faucet projection, curve, and height vs basin depth drive splash, posture, and usable space.

  • Landscaping

    Mapped plant roles — visual, aromatic, medicinal, fruit-bearing — plus sustainable irrigation for long-term care.

Proposed solutions

Practical villa UX moves — hide infrastructure with access, choose materials by use zone, refine washroom comfort, and design nature as part of daily living.

Utilities with access

  • Wood or stone-textured covers that blend with the façade.
  • Color-matched modular PVC panels with snap-fit access.
  • Vertical gardens or bamboo trellis to hide lines organically.

Doors

  • PVC for washrooms and utility areas.
  • Wood-grain PVC laminates or matte textures.
  • Custom handles and trims for a premium finish.

Bath, clean & spacious

  • Light palette to amplify airiness and natural light.
  • Matte anti-skid tiles; matched wall and floor tones.
  • Faucets ≥120mm projection, curved spout, height aligned to basin.

Nature as everyday UX

  • Layer privacy trees with low shrubs for depth.
  • Herb-lined pathways for a sensory walk.
  • Fruit corners and drip irrigation for lasting care.

Visual

Areca Palm, Frangipani, Hibiscus, Bougainvillea

Aromatic

Jasmine, Lavender, Lemongrass, Raat Ki Rani

Medicinal

Neem, Tulsi, Moringa, Curry Leaf

Fruit-bearing

Guava, Pomegranate, Lemon, Banana, Papaya

Process improvements

Delivery didn’t stop at design suggestions — it included execution, maintenance planning, and ethics-led sales experience for families visiting the site.

Execution & maintenance

  • Detailed execution plan across functional experience, accessibility, and landscaping.
  • Long-term maintenance plan including drip irrigation and accessible utility covers.

Sales & marketing SOPs

  • Prioritize completing the model villa with landscaping and plants for display.
  • Budget free rides so prospects can experience the premise comfortably.
  • Center elderly visitors and women of the family — belonging first in every sales visit.

Successful metrics

Outcomes reflect adoption of villa UX, landscaping-as-experience, and ethics-led site-visit process across Iqon West.

  • ~95% Phase 1 villas sold by 2025
  • ~50% Phase 2 sold while still in pre-launch
